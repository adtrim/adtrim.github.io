Why it exists

Most ad-trim workflows are destructive, slow, and approximate. This one isn't.

50–150ms vs 500–1200ms

Seeks that don't make you wait.

mpv-backed playback lands on a keyframe in milliseconds. The alternative (re-decoding from the nearest I-frame every time) takes whole seconds. You'll feel the difference on every nudge.

±1frame microsecond-precision splits

Frame-accurate, not "close enough."

Splits are stored as int64 microsecond offsets and refined against the actual frame index. Jog left/right with , / . until the cut sits exactly between the last frame of the show and the first frame of the ad.

0bytes written to the source

Non-destructive by default.

Edits live in a sidecar .adt.json. Trash it and the original recording is byte-identical to what your DVR wrote. Export builds a new merged MP4; the source is never touched.

Features

Built for the people who actually watch the library.

Non-destructive sidecar edits

Every split, label, and refinement is written to a .adt.json next to the recording. Source files are read-only.

Frame-accurate timeline

Microsecond-resolution playhead, snap-to-frame on splits, and zoom from full-clip to single-frame in one keystroke.

Auto-refine split boundaries

Use FFmpeg-backed silence, black-frame, and scene-change signals to snap rough chapter boundaries onto cleaner cut frames.

One-click merged export

Export the parts you keep as one MP4, with your segment names preserved as chapters. Use supported NVIDIA, Intel, or AMD hardware encoding, or choose software encoding.

Keyboard-first editing

← → seek · [ ] step 1 second
, . step frame by frame
S split · K toggle keep
R refine · E export

Made for Plex & Jellyfin libraries

Keep the show, season, and episode information from your recording's filename. Trimmed MP4s get an [AdTrim] label so you can tell them apart from the original in your library folder.

Privacy

Your recordings stay private.

AdTrim does not collect usage analytics or upload your recordings, filenames, or hardware information. Update checks download release information from GitHub Pages and compare versions on your computer. GitHub Pages logs IP addresses for security.

Automatic checks are on by default. You can turn them off during setup or under Help → Automatically check for updates. This also stops automatic security-update alerts. Help → Check for updates remains available whenever you choose to use it.
